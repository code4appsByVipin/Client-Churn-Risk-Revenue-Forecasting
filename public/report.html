1. Problem and approach

I study a managed IT services portfolio that provides infrastructure management, cloud migration, security and 24/7 support. I estimate which clients may not renew, review the signals behind their scores, forecast revenue and plan retention work.

The portfolio has about 10 million transaction rows, 12% missing financial values and an 18% annual churn rate. The transaction volume is a scale assumption. I define churn as not renewing within 12 months and interpret “reduce churn by 5%” as a five-percentage-point change, from 18% to 13%. All amounts are in USD.

2. Data preparation

I would use SQL or Spark to group billing, support and service events into one row per client per month. At 200 clients and about 25 monthly snapshots, this creates roughly 5,000 client-month records. The 10 million raw transactions set the scale; they are not loaded in this project.

Example query. This SQL groups billing, support and service events by client and month. I did not run it on real data.

Missing financial values. I keep incomplete records and fill gaps with training-period medians from similar service and contract groups. I also retain a flag for missing financial values.

Churn labels. I give churn cases more weight and check recall and PR-AUC rather than accuracy alone. At an 18% churn rate, an all-renew prediction is 82% accurate but finds no churners. I train on earlier months and test on later months.

8. Conclusion, limitations and next steps

Service quality and falling spend are the clearest signals in this analysis. About 11.6 exits are expected in the next 3 months, with 60 clients flagged for review. The 12-month forecast places about $8.3M at risk. I would check account context before acting.

Next steps

  1. Re-run the analysis on real billing, support and contract data. Confirm the renewal definition and data quality.
  2. Pilot actions on part of the flagged group and compare renewal and revenue outcomes.
  3. Track churn, saves and revenue monthly, then review the assumptions with finance and service teams.
Limitations. The client records are simulated and do not describe real clients. I assume yearly risk is spread evenly across months. The forecast leaves out new clients, expansion, price changes, currency changes and revenue losses unrelated to churn.
